Steel and cement are the backbone of any construction job — and among its biggest material costs. For a small project such as a shop, an extra floor or a warehouse extension, buying them well means getting the right grade, the right quantity and material that is stored properly until it is used.

## Get the quantities from your engineer

Do not estimate steel and cement by eye. Your structural engineer or contractor should give you:

- Steel quantity by diameter (for example 8 mm, 10 mm, 12 mm, 16 mm)
- Cement quantity in bags, and the type
- A schedule of when each lot is needed on site

Buying in stages as work progresses reduces storage problems and wastage.

## TMT bars: what to specify

- **Grade:** your engineer will specify the grade (for example Fe 500 or Fe 500D). Buy exactly what is specified.
- **Brand and certification:** ask for BIS-certified bars from a reputed manufacturer, and a test certificate for larger lots.
- **Weight:** bars are usually sold by weight. Ask how weighment is done and get a weighbridge slip for large quantities.

## Cement: types and freshness

- **OPC** (ordinary Portland cement) and **PPC** (Portland pozzolana cement) are the common types; use what your engineer specifies for each part of the work.
- Check the **manufacturing date** printed on the bags. Cement loses strength if stored too long — ask the supplier for recent stock.
- Bags should be intact, not torn or hardened.

## Compare delivered prices

Freight changes the real price of heavy material.

1. Ask whether the quote is ex-godown or delivered to your site.
2. Ask whether unloading is included.
3. Check whether trucks can reach your site, and plan for narrow lanes.

## Checking what arrives

- Count cement bags and check dates before signing the delivery note.
- Check steel for markings and diameter, and weigh a sample bundle if in doubt.
- Reject material that is rusted heavily, damaged or wet.

## Storing material on site

- Keep cement off the ground on a raised platform, covered, away from moisture, and use older stock first.
- Keep steel off the ground and covered to limit rusting.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying everything at once for a long job.** Cement can lose strength while it waits; buy in stages.
- **Accepting material without checking.** Count bags, check dates and look at bar markings before signing the delivery note.
- **Storing cement on bare ground.** Moisture from the floor hardens bags from below; use a raised, covered platform.
- **Changing the specified grade to save money.** Always check with your engineer before accepting a different grade or type.
- **Ignoring freight in comparisons.** For heavy material, delivery can decide which quote is really cheaper.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Steel quantity by diameter and the grade specified
- Cement quantity, type and preferred brands
- Site location and access for trucks
- Delivery schedule (all at once or in stages)
- Whether unloading is needed and whether you need GST invoices
