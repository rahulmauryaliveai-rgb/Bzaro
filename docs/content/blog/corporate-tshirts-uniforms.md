Corporate T-shirts and staff uniforms put your brand on people's backs every day. A shirt that shrinks, fades or fits badly after two washes does the opposite of what you intended. The good news: most problems are avoidable if you specify the garment clearly and approve a sample before bulk production.

## Choose the right fabric

- **Cotton** is comfortable and breathable — good for everyday wear in Indian weather — but may shrink if not pre-shrunk.
- **Polyester and blends** (poly-cotton) hold colour and shape better, dry quickly and suit sports events and field staff.
- **Pique knit** is the textured fabric used for polo T-shirts; it looks more formal than plain jersey.
- For uniforms such as shirts and trousers, ask about fabric composition, colour fastness and how it behaves after repeated washing.

## What GSM tells you

GSM (grams per square metre) indicates fabric weight.

- Lighter fabric is cooler but can look thin and see-through in light colours.
- Heavier fabric feels premium and lasts longer but is warmer.

Ask the manufacturer to show two or three fabrics at different GSMs so you can feel the difference.

## Size break-up and fit

Get sizes right before production — it is the most common problem.

1. Collect sizes from your team instead of guessing an average spread.
2. Ask for the manufacturer's **size chart** with actual measurements, since sizes vary between makers.
3. Order a few extras in common sizes for new joiners.

## Printing or embroidery?

- **Screen printing** is economical for larger quantities of the same design.
- **Digital or heat-transfer printing** suits small quantities or multi-colour designs.
- **Embroidery** looks premium and lasts well, and suits logos on polos and uniforms.

Share your logo in vector format and confirm the size and position of each print or embroidery.

## Samples, minimums and timelines

- Ask for a **sample garment** in the chosen fabric and branding before bulk production.
- Ask for the **minimum order per design and colour**, and prices at two or three quantities.
- Agree a delivery date in writing, especially if the order is for an event.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Skipping the wash test.** Wash the sample two or three times and check shrinkage, colour and print before approving bulk production.
- **Guessing sizes.** Collect actual sizes from people; a spread based on assumptions leaves you with piles of unusable sizes.
- **Oversized logos on polos.** A neat chest logo usually looks more professional than a large print, and costs less to embroider.
- **Tight timelines for events.** Leave a buffer between the promised delivery date and your event.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Garment type (round-neck T-shirt, polo, shirt, trousers, jacket)
- Fabric, GSM range and colours
- Quantity with size break-up
- Printing or embroidery, number of positions and logo files
- Sample requirement and the date you need delivery
- Delivery location and GSTIN for invoicing
