Personal protective equipment only protects people who wear it. Gear that is uncomfortable, the wrong size or wrong for the job ends up in a locker. Buying PPE well means starting from the actual hazards on your floor and choosing items workers will keep on through a full shift.

## Start with the hazards

Walk the shop floor and list what can hurt people:

- Falling or heavy objects, sharp edges, hot surfaces
- Chemicals, oils and solvents
- Flying particles from grinding or cutting
- Noise, dust and fumes
- Electrical work

Each hazard points to the kind of protection needed.

## Safety shoes

- **Toe protection** (steel or composite toe) where heavy items are handled.
- **Sole properties** — slip resistance, oil resistance, heat resistance or puncture resistance as your floor requires.
- Electrical work may need footwear rated for it.
- Order a sizing sample set; ill-fitting shoes cause blisters and get abandoned.

## Gloves

Different jobs need different gloves:

- Cut-resistant gloves for sheet metal and glass
- Chemical-resistant gloves for handling solvents or cleaners
- Heat-resistant gloves for hot work and welding
- General-purpose coated gloves for material handling

One glove type rarely suits a whole factory.

## Helmets, eyewear and more

1. **Helmets** — check fit, adjustable harness and ventilation for hot conditions.
2. **Safety glasses or goggles** — for grinding, cutting and chemical splashes; anti-fog helps in humid weather.
3. **Ear protection** in noisy areas.
4. **Masks or respirators** where there is dust or fumes, matched to the hazard.

## Certification

Ask which standards each item is certified to and ask for documentation. Genuine certification matters more than brand names.

## Comfort and replacement

- Involve a few workers in trials before bulk orders.
- PPE wears out: plan replacement cycles and keep stock of common sizes.
- Train people on correct use and care.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying one type of glove for everything.** A glove that protects from cuts may do nothing against chemicals or heat.
- **Ignoring comfort in summer.** Heavy, unventilated gear gets removed in the heat. Trial items during your hottest months, not just in a cool office.
- **Not replacing worn gear.** A cracked helmet or a shoe with worn soles gives false confidence. Set a replacement schedule and inspect regularly.
- **No training.** Many injuries happen with PPE that was worn wrongly — loose helmet straps, goggles on the forehead. A short demonstration at the start of each shift season helps.
- **Leaving visitors out.** Keep spare helmets, glasses and shoe covers for visitors, drivers and contractors entering the shop floor.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Items needed with quantities and size break-up
- Hazards and work environment for each item
- Certification requirements
- Whether you need a monthly supply arrangement
- Delivery location and GSTIN
