Slow or patchy internet in an office is more than an annoyance — it stalls calls, billing and every cloud tool the team depends on. Most small-office network problems come from a setup that grew piece by piece: a home router, a few extra devices plugged in, Wi-Fi that does not reach the back cabin. Planning it once, properly, avoids years of daily friction.

## Start with how the office uses the network

List what depends on it:

- Number of people, laptops and phones on Wi-Fi at the same time
- Video calls, cloud software, large file transfers
- CCTV recorders, biometric attendance, printers and billing machines
- Any server or storage device in the office

This decides the size of the internet plan, the router and the switches.

## The internet connection

- Ask your internet provider about the speed actually delivered, not just the plan name, and whether the plan has any usage cap.
- For businesses that cannot go offline, a **second connection from a different provider** with automatic failover is worth considering.
- A **static IP** may be needed if you run remote access to CCTV or servers.

## Router and firewall

A business-grade router or firewall handles more users, supports separate networks and gives better control.

- Ask for **separate networks for staff and guests** so visitors do not reach your printers and files.
- Ask about basic security features and whether the vendor will set strong admin passwords and keep firmware updated.

## Switches and wired points

Desks with fixed computers, printers and CCTV recorders work better on wired connections.

- Count wired points needed now and add spare ports for growth.
- **PoE (Power over Ethernet) switches** can power IP cameras and Wi-Fi access points through the network cable, saving separate power points.

## Wi-Fi coverage

One router in a corner rarely covers a whole office, especially with walls and cabins.

1. Ask for **access points** placed for coverage, managed as one network so devices roam smoothly.
2. Ask the vendor to check coverage in every cabin and meeting room after installation.

## Cabling and the rack

- **Structured cabling** — cables run neatly to a central rack, each point labelled — makes future changes and fault-finding far easier.
- Ask what category of cable is being used and whether points will be tested.
- A small wall-mounted rack keeps the router, switches and patch panel safe and tidy.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using a home router for the whole office.** It struggles once twenty devices connect.
- **Unlabelled cables.** Every fault then starts with an hour of tracing wires.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Office size, number of cabins and floors
- Number of users and devices, and number of wired points needed
- Current internet provider and whether you want a backup line
- Whether CCTV or other systems will share the network
- Whether you need cabling, installation and annual maintenance
- Preferred completion date
