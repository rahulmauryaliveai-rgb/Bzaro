Cooling a shop, showroom or office is very different from cooling a bedroom. Doors open constantly, lights and computers add heat, and many people share the space. An AC that is too small runs flat out and still cannot keep up; one that is too big wastes money. Getting the size and type right is where the savings are.

## Size for heat load, not just floor area

Tonnage depends on more than square feet:

- Room size and ceiling height
- Sun exposure and glass frontage
- Number of people at peak times
- Lights, computers, kitchen equipment and machines
- How often doors open

Ask the supplier or HVAC contractor to do a **heat load calculation** or site survey rather than using a rule of thumb.

## Choosing the type

- **Split ACs** — common for cabins and small offices.
- **Cassette ACs** — fit in the false ceiling and spread air in all directions; popular in shops and open offices.
- **Ductable units** — hide the equipment and deliver air through ducts to several points; suit larger spaces.
- **VRF/VRV systems** — serve many rooms from central outdoor units; used in larger offices and buildings.

## Inverter technology and energy ratings

- **Inverter ACs** adjust compressor speed and usually save power in spaces with varying load.
- **Energy ratings** (star labels) help compare efficiency. For long daily running hours in commercial use, efficiency matters a lot over the years.

## Installation quality

Poor installation undoes a good AC.

1. Ask what is included — copper piping length, stand, drain pipe, electrical work.
2. Check the outdoor unit location has ventilation and is allowed by the building.
3. Ask for proper insulation of pipes and a stable electrical supply, with a stabiliser if needed.

## Service and warranty

- Ask about warranty on the unit and compressor.
- Ask about annual maintenance contracts — filter cleaning and servicing before summer keep efficiency up.
- Check how quickly service engineers respond in your area.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Sizing by a rule of thumb.** A shop with glass frontage and constant footfall needs far more cooling than a closed cabin of the same size. Insist on a site survey.
- **Putting the outdoor unit where it cannot breathe.** An outdoor unit squeezed into a closed duct or facing another unit's hot air works harder and fails sooner.
- **Skipping the stabiliser or electrical check.** Voltage swings damage compressors. Ask whether your supply needs protection and whether the wiring can take the load.
- **No pre-summer service.** Clogged filters and coils quietly raise power bills. Book servicing before the peak season, when technicians are easier to get.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Area, ceiling height and type of space
- Number of people, equipment and glass frontage
- AC type preference, or ask for a recommendation
- Installation requirements and building constraints
- Your location and required date
