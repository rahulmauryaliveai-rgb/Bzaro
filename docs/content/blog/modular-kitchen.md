A modular kitchen is used several times a day for years, in heat, steam and moisture. Two quotes for "the same" kitchen can differ widely because the materials inside differ — and you only discover the difference when a shutter sags or a cabinet swells. Asking the right questions before signing protects you.

## Layout and measurements

- Ask for a **site measurement** by the maker, not your own estimate.
- Discuss the layout — straight, L-shaped, U-shaped or parallel — based on the space, windows and plumbing.
- Plan positions for the sink, hob, chimney, fridge, water purifier and power points before the design is final.

## Carcass: the part you do not see

The carcass is the box of each cabinet.

- Ask what board is used (for example, a specific grade of plywood, HDHMR or particle board) and its thickness.
- Ask which board is used for **sink cabinets**, which face constant moisture.
- Ask whether edges are properly banded to keep moisture out.

## Shutters and finishes

- Laminate, acrylic, membrane, PU, glass — each has a different look, durability and cost.
- Look at real samples in daylight, not only brochures.
- Ask how each finish handles heat and cleaning.

## Hardware quality

Hinges, channels, drawer systems and baskets take daily wear.

1. Ask which hardware brands are used.
2. Ask whether drawers and hinges are soft-close.
3. Ask for the hardware warranty separately.

## Countertop, appliances and accessories

Clarify what is included: countertop material, sink, tap, hob, chimney, built-in oven, tall units, corner solutions. Missing items are a common reason for final bills rising.

## Timelines, payments and warranty

- Ask for a written timeline from design sign-off to installation.
- Link payments to stages — advance, delivery, installation completion.
- Ask what the warranty covers and for how long, and who handles service.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing only from a 3D render.** Renders make every material look perfect. Touch real samples of the board and shutters.
- **Cheap boards under the sink.** This is where leaks happen; use the most water-resistant board you can afford there.
- **Too few power points.** Plan sockets for every appliance you use today and one or two more for later.
- **Paying most of the amount upfront.** Keep a meaningful part of the payment for after installation and checks.
- **Not planning ventilation.** A chimney needs a proper duct outlet; confirm the route before the design is final.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Location and kitchen dimensions (or ask for a site visit)
- Preferred layout and finish
- Appliances and accessories to include
- Budget range, if you have one
- When you need the kitchen installed
