When your business buys from suppliers, the GST invoice is more than a bill — it is what your accountant uses to claim input tax credit and keep your records straight. A small mistake on an invoice, such as a wrong GSTIN, can mean follow-ups, delays or lost credit. A quick check when the invoice arrives prevents most problems.

This is general guidance for buyers; for rules that apply to your business, speak to your chartered accountant or tax consultant.

## Give your details correctly before ordering

Share these with the supplier in writing when you place the order:

- Your registered business name, exactly as on your GST registration
- Your **GSTIN**
- Billing address and, if different, delivery address

Most invoice errors start with details given verbally or in a hurry.

## Check the supplier's details

- Supplier's name, address and **GSTIN** are printed.
- You can verify a GSTIN on the official GST portal to confirm it is active and matches the business name.

## Check the invoice content

1. **Invoice number and date**
2. **Description of goods or services** matching what you ordered
3. **HSN or SAC codes** for the items
4. **Quantity, rate and taxable value**
5. **Tax type** — CGST and SGST for supplies within your state, IGST for supplies from another state (your accountant can confirm what applies)
6. **Tax amounts and total** adding up correctly

## Match invoice to delivery

- Compare the invoice with the delivery challan and what actually arrived.
- Short supply, damaged goods or wrong items should be raised immediately, so the supplier can issue a correction.

## Keep records organised

- Send invoices to your accountant promptly, not at the end of the quarter.
- Ask your accountant how they check that supplier invoices appear in your GST records, and flag mismatches early.
- Keep copies with the purchase order, delivery challan and payment proof.

## Choose suppliers who invoice properly

A supplier who issues correct GST invoices without being chased is usually well organised in other ways too. On Bzaro you can mention in your requirement that you need a GST invoice, so suppliers know up front.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Accepting invoices in a personal name.** For business purchases, the invoice should be in your business name with your GSTIN if you plan to claim credit.
- **Waiting until the quarter ends to check.** Errors are easiest to correct soon after the sale.
- **Paying in cash for large purchases without a proper invoice.** It weakens your records and your ability to raise a claim if goods are faulty.
- **Mixing up billing and delivery addresses.** When they differ, make sure both are shown correctly.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Your GSTIN and billing details
- Whether you need a GST invoice (for business purchases, you usually do)
- Delivery address, if different from billing
