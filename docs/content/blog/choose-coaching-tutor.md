Choosing tuition is a big decision for parents — it takes your child's time, your money and often a daily commute. The right tutor builds confidence and fills gaps; the wrong one just adds hours. A few direct questions before you enrol make the choice much clearer.

## Start with what your child needs

- Is it one weak subject, or overall support?
- Does your child learn better one-to-one or in a small group?
- Is the goal school exams, a competitive exam, or building basics?

Knowing this helps you decide between a home tutor, a small batch or a larger coaching centre.

## Questions to ask any tutor or centre

1. **Experience with your board and class** — CBSE, ICSE or state board, and the specific class level.
2. **Who actually teaches?** At larger centres, ask whether the teacher you meet is the one who will teach the batch.
3. **Batch size** — smaller batches allow more individual attention.
4. **Trial class** — ask whether your child can attend one or two classes before you pay.
5. **Tests and progress** — how often are tests held, and how are results shared with parents?
6. **Doubt clearing** — is there time for individual questions, in class or separately?
7. **Study material** — is it provided, and is it included in the fee?

## Home tutor, centre or online?

- **Home tutors** give one-to-one attention and save travel, but check references carefully.
- **Coaching centres** offer structured batches, peer learning and regular tests.
- **Online classes** save travel and widen choice; they work best for children who can stay focused on a screen.

## Fees and practical details

- Ask what the fee covers — classes per week, material, tests.
- Ask about the payment schedule and the policy if you stop midway.
- Check timings against school hours and travel time. A tired child learns little.

## Safety and comfort

For home tutors, ask for identification and references. For centres, look at the classroom, seating and washrooms. Ask your child after the trial class how they felt — their comfort matters.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing only by the centre's advertised results.** Toppers' photos tell you little about how an average student in a large batch is taught. Ask about your child's batch, not the best one.
- **Too many hours on top of school.** A tired child in the fourth class of the day learns little. Leave time for rest, play and self-study.
- **Paying for the full year upfront without a trial.** Attend trial classes first, and prefer shorter payment cycles until you are sure.
- **Not talking to the child.** Ask after a few weeks whether they understand the classes and feel comfortable asking questions. Their answer matters more than the brochure.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Class, board and subjects
- Home, centre or online preference, and your area
- Preferred days and timings
- Batch or one-to-one
- When you want to start
