For a small food brand — spices, snacks, dry fruits, tea — the pouch is both protection and advertisement. It has to keep the product fresh, be safe for food contact, carry the required information and still look good on a shelf. Getting the specification right before printing avoids an expensive reprint.

## Choose the pouch type

- **Standing (stand-up) pouches** display well on shelves and suit snacks, spices and dry fruits.
- **Three-side and centre-seal pouches** are economical for sachets and refill packs.
- **Zipper pouches** let customers reseal the pack — useful for products used over several days.
- **Window pouches** show the product; ask whether the window affects shelf life for light-sensitive items.

## Barrier and material

Food spoils from moisture, oxygen and light. Pouches are made of layered films (laminates) to block these.

- Tell the supplier your product, its oil or moisture content, and the shelf life you need.
- Ask what laminate structure they recommend and why.
- Ask whether the inks and materials are **food-grade and suitable for direct food contact**.

## Labels and printed information

Packaged food must carry certain information on the label. Check the current labelling requirements from FSSAI or your food regulatory consultant — they cover items such as the product name, ingredients, net quantity, dates, manufacturer details and licence number, among others.

Leave space for **batch numbers and dates** that change with every run — these are often printed or stamped separately.

## Printing process and run size

- **Rotogravure printing** gives excellent quality for large runs but has cylinder costs and higher minimums.
- **Digital printing** suits small brands testing the market with smaller quantities and several designs.
- Ask for minimum order quantity, cylinder or setup charges, and price at two or three quantities.

## Proofs and samples

1. Approve a colour proof before production.
2. Ask for sample pouches to test filling, sealing and how the pouch looks when full.
3. Check the seal strength and zipper on your own filling setup.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Printing a large run before testing.** Fill, seal and store a few sample pouches for some weeks to see how the product holds up.
- **Forgetting space for changing details.** Batch numbers and dates change every run; leave a clear area for them.
- **Mismatched pouch and product weight.** A pouch that looks half empty on the shelf, or bulges when full, hurts your brand. Test with the real product before finalising the size.
- **Ignoring the zipper and seal area in design.** Text and logos placed where the pouch is sealed or cut get distorted.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Product, pack weight and shelf life needed
- Pouch type, size, zipper or window
- Number of designs and colours, and whether artwork is ready
- Quantity per design and delivery date
- Whether you also need labels or printed sachets
- Delivery location and GSTIN
