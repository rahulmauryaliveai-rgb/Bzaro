Many small manufacturers and traders ship goods that do not fill a whole truck — a few cartons, a pallet or two. Part-load (PTL or LTL) transport shares truck space with other consignments. It can be economical, but charges, transit times and handling vary a lot between transporters.

## Part load or full load?

- **Part load** suits smaller consignments and regular dispatches to many cities.
- **Full load** makes sense when you fill most of a truck, or goods are fragile and should not be handled repeatedly at hubs.

If your volume is close to a full load, ask for both quotes.

## How freight is charged

Ask how the transporter calculates the charge:

- By actual weight, or by volumetric weight (size) — whichever is higher
- Minimum chargeable weight per consignment
- Extra charges: door pickup, door delivery, loading and unloading, fuel surcharge, handling of odd-sized items

A low base rate with many extras can cost more than a higher all-inclusive rate.

## Transit time and network

- Ask for transit times to your key destinations, not just "all India".
- Ask whether they deliver to the door or to a branch the receiver must visit.
- Ask how you can track shipments.

## Packing and handling

Part-load goods are loaded and unloaded several times.

1. Pack in strong cartons, and palletise if possible.
2. Mark cartons with consignee details, handling marks and count (for example 1 of 5).
3. Ask the transporter about their packing requirements and liability for damage.

## Insurance and claims

- Ask whether transit insurance is included or available, and at what cost.
- Understand the claim process and time limits before you need it.

## Documents

For goods movement within India, check your **e-way bill** obligations and GST invoice requirements with your accountant. Ask the transporter what documents they need at pickup.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Comparing only the per-kilogram rate.** Minimum charges, door delivery and handling fees can double the real cost of a small consignment.
- **Weak packing for multi-point handling.** Part-load goods change trucks at hubs. Cartons that are fine for a direct delivery may not survive.
- **Missing documents.** A consignment held because of an e-way bill or invoice issue loses days. Keep paperwork ready at pickup.
- **Not informing the receiver.** Share the docket number and expected date with your customer so they are ready to receive and inspect the goods.
- **Signing clean delivery on damaged goods.** Ask your receiver to note visible damage on the delivery receipt; it matters for claims.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Pickup location and destinations
- Weight, dimensions and number of packages per shipment
- Type of goods, and whether they are fragile
- Frequency (one-time or regular)
- Door pickup and door delivery needs, and insurance
