For a restaurant, quality and consistency of spices, rice, pulses and flour decide how every dish tastes. For a retail store, they decide whether customers come back. Buying in bulk can lower costs — but only if what arrives matches what you agreed and stays fresh until you use it.

## Specify the grade

- **Rice:** variety, grain length, age (aged rice cooks differently) and whether it is sortex-cleaned.
- **Pulses:** variety, polished or unpolished, and size.
- **Spices:** whole or ground, origin if it matters to you, and colour or pungency for chillies.
- **Flour:** type, grind and intended use.

Use the words your trade uses, or share a sample of what you currently buy.

## Always ask for samples

1. Ask for samples before the first bulk order.
2. Cook or test them the way you actually use them.
3. Keep a reference sample and compare each new delivery against it.

## Packing and freshness

- Ask about pack sizes — sacks, bags or consumer packs for retail.
- Check the **packing date and best-before date** on each lot.
- Buy quantities you can use within a sensible time; ground spices lose aroma faster than whole spices.

## Food safety and documents

- Ask for the supplier's **FSSAI licence or registration number** and check it.
- For retail sale, packaged goods must carry the required label information; check current rules with FSSAI or your consultant.
- Ask about cleaning and quality checks at the supplier's end.

## Storage at your end

- Store off the floor, in a cool, dry place away from strong smells.
- Use airtight containers for opened stock.
- Follow first-in, first-out so older stock is used first.
- Check regularly for moisture and pests.

## Pricing and regular supply

Prices of agricultural products move with seasons and markets. For regular needs, ask suppliers about monthly or quarterly supply arrangements and how price changes will be communicated.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Judging only by price per kilogram.** Cheaper rice that breaks while cooking, or chilli powder with weak colour, costs you more in the kitchen and in customer complaints.
- **Skipping the sample for every new lot.** Quality can change between crops and batches even from the same supplier. Compare each delivery with your reference sample before accepting it.
- **Buying ground spices far ahead.** Whole spices keep their aroma much longer. If you have a grinder, buying whole and grinding in smaller batches keeps flavour consistent.
- **Storing stock near the kitchen heat.** Warmth and humidity speed up spoilage and attract pests. Keep the store room cool, dry and separate.
- **No written specification.** A short note of variety, grade and pack size agreed with the supplier avoids arguments later when a lot does not match.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Items with grade, variety and pack size
- Monthly quantity for each
- Sample requirement
- Delivery location and frequency
- Whether you need GST invoices and FSSAI details
