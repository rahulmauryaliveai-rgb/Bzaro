Your sign board works for you every hour the shop is open — and after dark, if it is lit. A good sign is readable from the road, looks professional and lasts through years of sun and rain. A poor one fades, peels or cannot be read from across the street.

## Types of shop signage

- **Flex boards** — economical and quick, but fade and tear sooner outdoors.
- **ACP (aluminium composite panel) boards** — durable, clean-looking backgrounds, often with letters mounted on top.
- **Acrylic 3D letters** — premium look, can be lit from inside or behind.
- **LED signage** — lit letters or backlit boxes that stand out at night.
- **Glow sign boxes** — lit boxes with printed faces, common in markets.

## Size and readability

1. Measure the space available on the frontage.
2. Consider the distance and angle from which people will read it — from across the road, from a passing vehicle.
3. Keep the text short: business name, what you sell and a phone number. Too much text is unreadable at a distance.
4. Choose strong contrast between letters and background.

## Lighting

- Front-lit, back-lit or letters lit from inside — ask the signage maker to show examples at night.
- Ask about LED quality, power consumption and the warranty on lighting.
- A timer switch saves power and turns the sign on and off automatically.

## Weather and installation

- Outdoor materials should resist sun and rain; ask what the maker uses.
- Installation at height needs a proper frame and safe fixing — ask who installs and whether scaffolding is included.
- Check that wiring is weather-protected.

## Permissions

Many cities and building societies have rules on outdoor signage size, lighting and placement. Check with your landlord, market association or local authority before installation.

## Design and proofs

Share your logo in vector format and ask for a mock-up on a photo of your shop front. It is much easier to fix a design on screen than on a finished board.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Too much text.** A board crowded with every product you sell is read by nobody. Name, category and a phone number are usually enough.
- **Choosing colours on a screen.** Check a printed or physical sample outdoors; sunlight changes how colours look.
- **Cheap lighting.** Uneven or flickering LEDs make a shop look neglected at night. Ask about LED quality and warranty.
- **Ignoring maintenance.** Dust and failed LEDs make a sign look old quickly. Plan cleaning and ask whether the maker services what they install.
- **Skipping permission checks.** Removing or altering a sign after a complaint costs more than asking first.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Shop location and frontage size (with a photo if possible)
- Sign type preference, or ask for options
- Lit or unlit, and text/logo content
- Installation height and access
- Required date
