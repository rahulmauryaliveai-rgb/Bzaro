Fasteners are small and cheap per piece, which is exactly why they get specified carelessly. But a bolt of the wrong grade in a machine, or a lot with a slightly different thread, can stop a production line or cause a failure. Clear specifications turn fasteners into a routine repeat order instead of a recurring problem.

## Size and thread

- **Diameter and length** — for metric fasteners, written as M10 × 50, for example.
- **Thread pitch** — standard (coarse) or fine. Mixing them up means parts will not fit.
- **Metric or inch** — older or imported machines may use inch sizes.
- **Head type** — hex, socket head (Allen), countersunk, button head, and so on.

If you are replacing an existing part, share a sample or the drawing.

## Grade and strength

Bolts come in strength grades, usually marked on the head (for example 8.8 or 10.9 for metric bolts).

- Use the grade your design or machine manufacturer specifies — do not substitute a lower grade to save money.
- Match nuts to bolts of a compatible grade.
- For structural or safety-critical use, ask for test certificates.

## Material and coating

- **Mild steel and alloy steel** for general and high-strength use
- **Stainless steel** where corrosion resistance matters — food, outdoor, chemical environments
- **Coatings** such as zinc plating or hot-dip galvanising protect against rust; ask which suits your environment

## Standards and consistency

Ask which standard the fasteners follow (IS, DIN or ISO). Standardising on one reduces confusion between lots and suppliers.

## Packing and ordering

1. Ask whether prices are per piece, per hundred or per kilogram.
2. Ask how items are packed and labelled — clearly labelled boxes save time on the shop floor.
3. For regular use, agree a monthly schedule or a stock-holding arrangement with the supplier.

## Checking what arrives

- Check head markings for grade.
- Measure a few pieces with a gauge.
- Keep lots separate until checked, so a bad lot does not mix with good stock.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Substituting a lower grade to save money.** The saving is tiny compared with the cost of a failure.
- **Mixing lots on the shelf.** Keep each lot labelled until it is checked; mixed bins make faults impossible to trace.
- **Ordering without drawings or samples.** For special or non-standard parts, a drawing or sample avoids a whole wrong batch.
- **Ignoring corrosion.** Plain steel fasteners in outdoor or wet areas rust quickly; specify the coating or stainless grade upfront.
- **No reorder level.** Running out of one common bolt size can stop assembly. Set minimum stock levels for your most-used items.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Item list with type, size, thread, length and head type
- Grade and material, and coating
- Standard (IS, DIN, ISO) if you follow one
- Quantity per item and whether it is a monthly requirement
- Delivery location and GSTIN
