"Need LED lights, send best price." Suppliers receive messages like this every day, and they reply with a guess — or not at all. A clear requirement gets you faster replies, quotes you can compare, and suppliers who take you seriously. It takes two extra minutes to write.

## Name the product precisely

Say exactly what you want, using the words a supplier would use.

- Not "wires" but "copper house wire, 2.5 sq mm, FR, 90 m coils"
- Not "boxes" but "5-ply corrugated boxes, inner size 40 × 30 × 25 cm, brown, unprinted"

If you are not sure of the technical terms, describe what the product must do and where it will be used. Good suppliers will suggest the right specification.

## Give the specification that matters

For most products, a supplier needs to know:

- Size, capacity, grade or model
- Material and finish
- Brand preference, or "any reputed brand"
- Certifications required (for example ISI/BIS marks)
- Any sample or reference photo you can share

## Quantity and frequency

State the quantity for this order, and whether it is a one-time purchase or a monthly or yearly requirement. Repeat business changes how suppliers price and how much attention they give you.

## Delivery location and timeline

- Your city and area, or the site address for heavy goods
- The date by which you need the goods
- Whether you need installation or setup

Location matters because freight is often a large part of the cost, and many suppliers serve only certain regions.

## Commercial details

- Whether you need a GST invoice (and your GSTIN)
- Your preferred payment terms
- Whether you want the price including delivery

## Why you are buying

A line of context helps: "for our new office in Noida", "for resale in our shop", "for a school opening in April". Suppliers can then recommend better options.


## Mistakes to avoid

- **"Best price" with no details.** Suppliers cannot quote on it and serious ones may not reply.
- **Hiding the quantity.** Price depends on quantity; a rough range is better than nothing.
- **Leaving out the city.** Freight and supplier coverage depend on it.
- **No timeline.** Without a date, you get quotes that cannot deliver in time.

## A simple template

1. **Product:** name and type
2. **Specification:** size, grade, material, brand, certification
3. **Quantity:** this order, and monthly/yearly if repeat
4. **Delivery:** city/site and required date
5. **Extras:** installation, samples, packing
6. **Terms:** GST invoice, payment preference
7. **Purpose:** personal, business use or resale
