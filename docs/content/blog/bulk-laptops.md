When a small business buys laptops for a whole team, the temptation is to pick one model and order twenty. That can work — but it often means overpaying for some roles and under-equipping others. A little planning gets everyone a machine that fits their work and keeps support simple.

## Match the configuration to the job

Group your people by what they actually do.

- **Office and sales staff** (email, documents, browsers, video calls) rarely need high-end processors. Enough RAM and an SSD make the biggest difference to how fast the laptop feels.
- **Accounts and operations** may run accounting software and large spreadsheets — ask your software vendor for recommended specifications.
- **Design, video or engineering roles** need stronger processors, more RAM, sometimes a dedicated graphics card and a better screen.

Two or three standard configurations are usually enough. Fewer models means easier support and spares.

## The specifications that matter most

1. **SSD storage** — much faster than a hard disk; do not accept a hard disk as the main drive for a new work laptop.
2. **RAM** — ask whether it can be upgraded later, or whether it is soldered.
3. **Battery life** for people who travel or work from client sites.
4. **Weight and build** for people who carry the laptop daily.
5. **Ports** — check you have what you need for projectors, monitors and USB devices, or plan for adapters.

## New or refurbished?

Refurbished business-grade laptops can be good value for basic roles.

- Ask what **grade** the refurbished units are and what was replaced or tested.
- Ask for the **warranty period** and who honours it.
- Check whether the machine comes with a **genuine, licensed operating system**.

For roles where downtime is costly, new laptops with an on-site warranty are usually worth it.

## Warranty and support

- Ask whether the warranty is carry-in or **on-site**, and how quickly engineers visit in your city.
- Ask about accidental damage cover if your staff travel a lot.
- Keep a spare laptop or two, so one failure does not stop someone's work for a week.

## Software, licences and setup

Include software in the plan: operating system edition, office suite, antivirus, and any business software. Ask whether the supplier can pre-install and configure machines before delivery, and label them by user — it saves your team a day of setup.

## GST invoices and asset records

Ask for GST invoices in your company's name with your GSTIN, and a list of serial numbers. That list makes warranty claims and asset tracking much easier.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **One model for everyone.** It either overspends on basic roles or slows down your heavy users.
- **Ignoring the charger and accessories.** Ask whether bags, mice and docking stations are included, and order a few spare chargers — they are the first thing to go missing.
- **No record of serial numbers.** Without them, warranty claims and asset audits become guesswork.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Number of laptops per configuration, with the roles they are for
- Minimum processor, RAM, SSD and screen size for each group
- New or refurbished, and the warranty type you need
- Software to be pre-installed and licensing needs
- Delivery location and date, and GSTIN for invoicing
