Setting up a clinic means buying many different items at once — furniture, equipment, storage and waiting-area seating. Planning room by room and choosing materials that are easy to clean saves money and makes daily work smoother. Your clinical requirements should always come from the doctor or specialist running the clinic; this guide covers the buying side.

## Plan room by room

List what each space needs:

- **Reception and waiting** — seating, reception desk, storage for files
- **Consultation room** — doctor's desk and chairs, examination table, screen or curtain, stool
- **Procedure or dressing room** — trolleys, instrument cabinets, lighting
- **Storage** — medicine racks, cabinets, a separate space for consumables

## Furniture built for clinical use

- **Examination tables** — ask about height, adjustable backrest, upholstery and weight capacity.
- **Trolleys and cabinets** — stainless steel or powder-coated steel is easier to clean and disinfect.
- **Upholstery** — choose material that can be wiped and disinfected daily.
- **Waiting chairs** — sturdy, easy to clean, with some chairs with armrests for elderly patients.

## Basic equipment

Your equipment list depends on your specialty. For each item:

1. Ask for the model, specifications and what is included.
2. Ask about calibration needs, if any.
3. Ask for manuals and training on use.

Buy from suppliers who can support the equipment after sale.

## Service support and warranty

- Ask about warranty, on-site service and how quickly an engineer can visit.
- Ask whether spares and consumables are easily available.
- For equipment you depend on daily, ask about maintenance contracts.

## Documents and compliance

Keep invoices, warranty cards and any certificates that come with equipment. For registration and regulatory requirements for your clinic, check with your state health authority or a consultant — they vary by location and specialty.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying everything before the layout is final.** Measure each room and door width first; examination tables and cabinets that do not fit through a door are a common setup-week problem.
- **Choosing fabric upholstery.** Anything patients sit or lie on should be wipeable and able to take daily disinfection.
- **Ignoring the waiting area.** Patients often spend longer there than in the consultation room. Comfortable, sturdy seating and good ventilation shape their impression of the clinic.
- **Buying equipment without local service.** A good price means little if the nearest engineer is in another city. Ask for the service address and response time in writing.
- **No place for consumables.** Gloves, dressings and disposables need organised, dry storage close to where they are used; plan cabinets for them from the start.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Type of clinic and number of rooms
- Item list per room with quantities
- Specialty-specific equipment needed
- Installation, training and service requirements
- Location and the date you plan to open
