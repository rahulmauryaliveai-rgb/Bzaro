A welding machine is the heart of most small fabrication workshops — gates, grills, frames, racks and repairs. Choosing the right process and machine for your typical jobs, and buying consumables that match, affects quality, speed and running cost every day.

## Which welding process?

- **Arc (stick/MMA) welding** — simple, rugged and works outdoors. Suits general fabrication and repairs on mild steel.
- **MIG welding** — faster, cleaner welds for production work, especially on thinner sheets. Needs wire and shielding gas.
- **TIG welding** — precise, neat welds on stainless steel and aluminium, but slower and needs more skill.

Tell the supplier the materials and thicknesses you work with most often.

## Duty cycle matters

Duty cycle tells you how long a machine can weld continuously at a given current before it needs to cool. A workshop doing long, continuous jobs needs a higher duty cycle than one doing occasional repairs. Ask for the duty cycle at the current you normally use.

## Power supply

- Check whether the machine runs on **single-phase or three-phase** supply, and what your workshop has.
- Voltage fluctuation is common in many industrial areas; ask how the machine handles it.
- Inverter-based machines are lighter and more efficient; ask about their service support in your city.

## Consumables

- **Electrodes** for arc welding — type and size depend on the material and job.
- **MIG wire and gas** — ask about wire diameter, spool size and gas cylinder arrangements.
- **Tungsten electrodes and filler rods** for TIG.
- Store electrodes dry; damp electrodes give poor welds.

## Safety equipment

Welding without protection damages eyes and skin and creates fire risk.

1. Auto-darkening or standard welding helmet with the right shade
2. Leather gloves, apron and safety shoes
3. Ventilation or fume extraction for enclosed spaces
4. A fire extinguisher within reach

## Warranty and service

Ask about the warranty, whether spares such as torches and cables are available, and where repairs are done.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Buying on maximum current alone.** A high rating means little if the duty cycle at that current is short.
- **Storing electrodes in a damp corner.** Moist electrodes cause porous, weak welds; keep them in a dry box.
- **Undersized cables and poor earthing.** They cause unstable arcs and safety risks; follow the maker's recommendations.
- **Skipping eye protection for helpers.** People nearby also need protection from arc flash, not just the welder.
- **No fire watch.** Sparks travel; clear flammable material from the area and keep an extinguisher ready.

## What to include in your enquiry

- Welding process, or ask for a recommendation
- Materials and typical thicknesses
- Power supply available (single/three phase)
- Consumables and safety gear needed
- Your location and required date
