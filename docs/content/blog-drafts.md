## 1. Buying CCTV for a shop or office: a practical checklist

**Slug:** `cctv-buying-checklist-shop-office`
**Summary:** What to decide before you ask for CCTV quotes — camera type, recording days, storage and installation — so the quotes you get can actually be compared.
**Tag categories:** Safety, Security & Surveillance
**Cover alt:** Dome CCTV camera fixed to a shop ceiling

**Body:**

Most CCTV quotes are hard to compare because every installer assumes something different about your site. Ten minutes spent answering the questions below before you ask for prices will save you a lot of back-and-forth — and a system that records nothing useful on the day you need it.

## Start with what you need to see

Walk around the premises and list the spots that matter: the entrance, the cash counter, the stockroom, the parking or loading area. For each one, decide whether you need to **recognise a face** or only **see that someone was there**. Recognising faces at a door needs a closer, higher-resolution camera than a wide view of a warehouse floor.

- Entrance and counter: face-level detail, usually 2–4 MP is enough at short distance
- Open areas and parking: wider view; think about night-time lighting
- Outdoor cameras need a weather-proof rating, and night vision if the area is dark

## IP or HD analogue?

HD analogue cameras connect to a DVR over coaxial cable and are simple for a small shop with a handful of cameras. IP cameras connect to an NVR over a network cable, carry higher resolutions more easily and suit larger sites or multiple floors. Neither is "better" in every case — describe the site and let two or three installers recommend, then compare the complete package.

## Decide how many days of recording you want

This decides the hard disk size, which is where many quotes quietly differ. A shop might want 15 days; a warehouse with occasional audits might want 30. Ask each installer to state the disk size and the number of days it will actually hold at the resolution they are quoting.

## What a complete quote should list

1. Camera models and how many of each
2. Recorder (DVR or NVR) and its channel count — leave room for one or two more cameras later
3. Hard disk size and the recording days it gives
4. Cabling, connectors and power supply
5. Installation and configuration charges, including mobile viewing setup
6. Warranty, and who you call if a camera stops working

If a quote is missing any of these, ask before you compare prices.

## Before you pay

Ask to see live and recorded footage from your own phone before the installer leaves, check the night view of at least one outdoor camera, and make sure you — not only the installer — have the admin password for the recorder.

## 2. Corrugated boxes: choosing the right ply, size and supplier

**Slug:** `corrugated-boxes-ply-size-supplier-guide`
**Summary:** How to specify corrugated boxes so they protect your product without paying for strength you do not need — and what to ask a box manufacturer before a bulk order.
**Tag categories:** Packaging Materials & Supplies
**Cover alt:** Stack of brown corrugated shipping boxes in a warehouse

**Body:**

A box that is too weak costs you damaged goods and returns. A box that is too strong costs you money on every single shipment. Getting the specification right is the most useful thing you can do before asking manufacturers for prices.

## Measure the inside, not the outside

Box makers work to inner dimensions: length × width × height, in that order. Measure your product (or its inner pack) and add a little room for padding if you use it. Sending outer dimensions is the most common reason a first batch does not fit.

## Ply: how many layers you actually need

Corrugated board is made of alternating flat and fluted layers.

- **3-ply** — light products, short distances, little stacking
- **5-ply** — most shipped goods, moderate weight, courier and truck transport
- **7-ply** — heavy items, long-distance transport, high stacking in godowns

Weight and stacking matter more than the product's value. Tell the supplier what goes inside, how much it weighs, how it travels and how high the boxes are stacked — a good manufacturer will recommend the board and paper grade, not only the ply.

## Printing and finish

Plain brown boxes are the most economical. One- or two-colour printing of your logo and handling marks adds cost per box and usually a minimum run. If boxes go to retail shelves or are part of an unboxing experience, ask about better printing options — but price them separately so you can see the difference.

## Questions to ask before a bulk order

1. What board and paper grade will you use for this ply?
2. What is the minimum order, and the price at two or three quantities?
3. Can you send a sample for approval?
4. What is the lead time for the first order, and for repeat orders?
5. Is delivery included to my location?

## Plan for repeat orders

If you ship every week, a manufacturer who can hold a small buffer stock or deliver on a fixed schedule is worth more than the lowest one-time price. Mention your monthly quantity in the enquiry.

## 3. Rooftop solar for a small business: what to ask before you sign

**Slug:** `rooftop-solar-small-business-questions`
**Summary:** The questions that separate a clear rooftop solar quote from a confusing one — sizing, on-grid or hybrid, what is included, and after-sales service.
**Tag categories:** Solar & Renewable Energy
**Cover alt:** Solar panels on the flat roof of a commercial building

**Body:**

Rooftop solar can cut a shop's or small factory's power bill for many years — but only if the system is sized properly and installed by someone who will still pick up the phone after year one. These are the questions worth asking every installer.

## Start from your electricity bill

Keep your last few months' bills ready. The units you use each month, and when you use them, decide the system size far more than the size of your roof. Share the bills with installers and ask how they arrived at the capacity they are proposing.

## On-grid, off-grid or hybrid?

- **On-grid** systems work alongside the grid and are usually the simplest where power supply is regular
- **Hybrid** systems add batteries for backup during cuts
- **Off-grid** systems run independently of the grid

Explain how often you face power cuts and which equipment must keep running. Let the installer justify the choice.

## What should be included in the price

1. Panel make, type and wattage, and how many
2. Inverter make and capacity
3. Mounting structure — material and height
4. Wiring, earthing and lightning protection
5. Net-metering and approval paperwork, if the installer handles it
6. Monitoring app or display
7. Warranties: panels, inverter and workmanship — listed separately

Two quotes with the same kilowatt figure can differ a lot in what they include. Put them side by side against this list.

## After-sales service

Ask who cleans the panels and how often, what happens if generation drops, and how quickly they respond to a fault. A local installer with a service team in your city is often the safer choice.

## Before you sign

Ask for a site visit, a written generation estimate, and the names of two or three installations you can see or call. Check that the final agreement lists every item from the quote.
